THE PROBLEM

I always struggled with where to put my jewelry. My box was too small, so things ended up tangled or piled on top of it, and most of the stands sold online were too bulky for my minimal room. I wanted something functional, visually quiet and compact enough for a nightstand, a stand that organizes rings, earrings, bangles and necklaces while looking like a decorative object in its own right.

How might we design a jewelry stand that organizes everything without overwhelming a small space?

design goals
  • Hold and separate rings, earrings, bangles and necklaces
  • Small enough to live on a nightstand
  • Look intentional, whether it’s full or empty
  • Assemble by hand, with no hardware or glue

FOUR DIRECTIONS
tree-inspired
Too irregular and branch-like, which made jewelry harder to organize, and it felt visually cluttered.
minimal hook
Too simple, with not enough room for different kinds of pieces.
tiered circular
Too bulky for the compact look I wanted, and better suited to storage than display.
interlocking
Structurally the strongest. It needed a cleaner silhouette and a better fit with the tray, and that’s the direction I refined.
FROM CARDBOARD TO PLYWOOD
01
Cardboard prototype

I traced and cut the interlocking profile in cardboard to test proportions, height and how the two pieces slot together.

02
CAD + constraints

I fully constrained the curved profile and the octagonal base in Fusion 360, with R1.289 arcs, a 3.5 inch stand height and slot placement matched to the plywood thickness.

03
Rendering + assembly

I rendered the stand, the base and the full assembly in plywood to check proportions and fit before committing material.

04
Fabrication

I laser-cut the parts from plywood, then test-fit and refined the slots until the stand locked in without being too tight or too loose.

THE FINAL DESIGN

Two curved plywood pieces interlock at the center to form a four-armed stand, which slots into an octagonal base.

arms
Hold bangles and rings
curves
Let necklaces drape without tangling
base
Catches the smaller pieces, like earrings

It assembles by hand with no hardware, sits neatly on my nightstand, and I use it every day.

WHAT I LEARNED

This was my first time on the laser cutter, and the hardest part was the smallest detail: getting the interlocking stand to fit the base without being too tight or too loose. My first cuts came out with the base too large and some parts not cutting fully through. It taught me how much material thickness, kerf and test cuts matter before final fabrication.

WHAT I’D DO NEXT

Add a third vertical piece at a different height, to hold more jewelry and make the stand even more useful.
