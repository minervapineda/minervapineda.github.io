Jewelry Holder

Year
Spring 2026
Context
ME102
Team
Solo
Tools
Laser-cut plywood, CAD, rapid prototyping, fabrication

An interlocking plywood stand that keeps jewelry organized and doubles as a minimal decor piece.

2 photos

Design Challenge

My jewelry box was too small, and most stands sold online were too bulky for my minimal room. I wanted a holder that’s functional, visually minimal and compact enough for a nightstand, one that organizes rings, earrings, bangles and necklaces while doubling as a decorative object.

How might we design a jewelry stand that organizes everything without overwhelming a small space?

4 photos

Ideation

Explored four directions before landing on the final form:

- Tree-inspired: too irregular and visually cluttered

- Minimal hook: too simple, not enough space for different pieces

- Tiered circular: too bulky, better for storage than display

- Interlocking: the strongest structure, but it needed a cleaner silhouette and better tray fit

Process

I cut it in cardboard first, constrained everything in Fusion 360, rendered it to check proportions, then took it to the laser cutter.

my first time on the laser cutter

Final Result

2 photos

laser-cut plywood · no hardware

Two curved plywood pieces interlock at the center to form a four-armed stand, which slots into an octagonal base. The upper arms hold bangles and rings, necklaces drape down the curves, and the base catches smaller pieces like earrings. It assembles by hand with no hardware, sits neatly on a nightstand, and looks as good empty as it does full.

Reflection

The biggest challenge was getting the interlocking stand to fit the base without being too tight or too loose. My first cuts also came out with the base too large and some parts not cutting fully through the plywood. As my first laser-cutting project, it taught me how much material thickness, kerf and test cuts matter before final fabrication. A future version would add a third vertical piece at a different height to hold more jewelry.
