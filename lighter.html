THE PROBLEM

A lighter is one of the most ordinary objects there is, and it’s quietly hard to use. Most lighters are designed for one hand size and short use. They need a lot of thumb force and an awkward twist of the wrist, they put your thumb right beside the flame, and nothing about them tells you which way the flame will come out. For people with arthritis it’s a struggle to keep a grip, and people who use one every day end up with wrist strain from the repetition.

How might we make a lighter that is easy and safe to use with limited grip strength?

design requirements
  • Less wrist strain
  • A longer nozzle for safety
  • A clear flame direction
  • A better grip for most hands

FROM SKETCH TO FOAM

We explored a wide range of grips on paper first: what would be easiest to hold, where the thumb should land, and how the hand should wrap. Then we carved them in foam in the PRL, because a form only proves itself once it’s in your hand.

design space exploration
WHAT TESTING SHOWED

We put our foam models in the hands of people who use lighters monthly and every day.

what worked
  • An angled thumb reduced the strain of ignition
  • A thumb groove signaled exactly where to press
  • The wrist stayed neutral across hand sizes
  • A finger indent made the grip more stable
  • More distance between the nozzle and the fingers made it safer
what didn’t
  • Unfamiliar forms without clear signifiers confused first-time users
  • One diameter didn’t scale well across hand sizes
  • Overly bulbous forms felt uncomfortable and weren’t pocketable
  • Very specific finger ridges fought against a natural grip
DESIGN DECISIONS
01
Combining the best of two models.

No single model got everything right, so we combined Model 4’s natural curvature with Model 6’s stabilizing grip features. The result improved comfort and control without losing the intuitiveness people liked.

model 4 + model 6
02
A form that fits many hands.

Instead of sculpting the grip for one hand, we designed geometry that conforms across different grip styles, finger lengths and hand widths. Survey rankings and user feedback consistently pointed to the forms that felt natural, stable and safe.

03
Keeping fingers away from the flame.

Every user mentioned the flame. The final form sets the fingers about 1.5 inches farther back from the ignition zone, so the hand stays clear without any extra effort.

WHAT I LEARNED

Wearing a hand brace while I designed changed how I saw the problem. It turned “limited grip strength” from a phrase in a brief into something I could feel, and every decision after that came from what my own hand struggled with.

WHAT’S NEXT

Still in progress: 3D print the final form, choose the grip material, and test it with people who have limited grip strength.
