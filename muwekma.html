01 · CHALLENGE

The Muwekma Community Garden is rooted in Indigenous Traditional Ecological Knowledge and holds plants with deep cultural, food and medicinal value. That knowledge was not easy to explore on site, especially for tribal members wanting to connect with specific plants.

How might we make the garden easier to explore while keeping Indigenous knowledge at the center?

context

For the Muwekma Ohlone, the garden supports food sovereignty, and plant names support language revitalization. Our work needed to respect both.

02 · PROCESS

Most of the work was listening: learning from the garden team, researching each plant, then mapping it all.

step 01
Partnering with the community

Worked with the garden team to learn which plants mattered most and what was appropriate to share. Their capacity shaped the whole timeline.

step 02
Plant research

Gathered each plant’s common name, scientific name and traditional uses. The same three fields went on the signs and the map, so both read as one system.

step 03
Mapping in ArcGIS

Plotted plant locations, then built a StoryMap so people can learn each plant’s story without a guide.

03 · FINAL DESIGN

Each plant appears as a point on the garden map. Selecting one shows its common name, scientific name and traditional uses, the same layout as the physical signs, on site or from home.
