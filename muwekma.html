THE CONTEXT

The Muwekma Community Garden is more than a place for plants. It’s rooted in Indigenous Traditional Ecological Knowledge and holds plants with deep cultural, food and medicinal value. For the Muwekma Ohlone, the garden supports food sovereignty, and plant names support language revitalization. That knowledge wasn’t easy to explore on site, though, especially for tribal members wanting to connect with specific plants.

How might we make the garden easier to explore while keeping Indigenous knowledge at the center?

WHAT I DID

We split the work evenly as a team of five, so I worked on every stage.

partnership
Coordinated with the Muwekma garden team to learn which plants mattered most and what was appropriate to share
research
Gathered each plant’s common name, scientific name and traditional uses
mapping
Plotted plant locations in ArcGIS and built the interactive StoryMap
signage
Designed educational signs alongside the map, as one consistent system
funding
Helped secure funding to produce the signs
DESIGN PRINCIPLES
01
Share only what the community wants shared.

Most of the work was listening. Before designing anything, we learned from the garden team which plants and stories were appropriate to make public. Representing Indigenous knowledge carefully mattered more than representing all of it.

02
One system, on the ground and on your phone.

Every plant gets the same three fields: common name, scientific name and traditional uses. They appear identically on the physical signs and in the map, so the two read as one experience. Where the community shared it, the sign also carries the plant’s Chochenyo name.

03
Explore without a guide.

The StoryMap lets tribal members and visitors tap any plant and learn its story on their own, whether they’re standing in the garden or at home.

THE PROCESS
01
Partnering with the community

We worked closely with the Muwekma garden team. Scheduling around their capacity shaped the whole timeline, and that patience became part of the design.

02
Plant research

For each plant we gathered the common name, scientific name and traditional uses, the fields that would carry through to both the signs and the map.

03
Mapping in ArcGIS

We plotted each plant’s location in the garden, then built an ArcGIS StoryMap around it.

THE RESULT

An interactive StoryMap of the Muwekma Community Garden, where each plant appears as a point on the garden map. Selecting one shows its common name, scientific name and traditional uses, in the same layout as the physical signs.

  • Plant map and StoryMap built for the garden and tribal members
  • Educational signage designed alongside it
  • Funding secured for sign production

WHAT I LEARNED

Working with a community partner taught me that good design depends on trust and timing as much as on the tools. Environmental justice and cultural sovereignty are deeply intertwined, and design has to center the people most affected by it.

WHAT’S NEXT

Hand off the map and sign files to the garden team, keep mapping new plants, and add plant names in native languages.
