THE PROJECT

The tracheal acoustic monitor is a wearable sensor that listens to breathing sounds at the neck. The team already had a working wired prototype. The next challenge is turning it into a wireless device that’s comfortable, reliable and ready for a clinical trial.

THE CHALLENGE

A device like this lives at the intersection of three sets of constraints, and improving one often costs another.

physical
It has to fit the curve of the neck comfortably and keep the sensors in steady contact with the skin.
acoustic
The enclosure has to protect signal quality, not muffle or distort the breathing sounds.
electronic
Going wireless means fitting batteries and electronics into a small enclosure without crowding the sensor.

How might we make a medical wearable small and comfortable enough to forget you’re wearing, without compromising the signal it exists to capture?

WHAT I DO
  • Help evaluate tradeoffs between sensor fit, acoustics, enclosure size and electronics placement
  • Inform materials selection for the enclosure and the parts that touch the skin
  • Contribute to CAD refinement of the enclosure
  • Help build assembly and testing pipelines as the design moves toward production
  • Attend site visits to hospitals and medical device manufacturers
  • Work alongside Stanford Medicine researchers and engineering consultants
